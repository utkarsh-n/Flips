Flips math and learning-flow review
Review method: read-only inspection of App/BitsView.swift, App/Theme.swift,
App/LogicView.swift, App/BasesView.swift, App/DiscoverView.swift and App/RadixApp.swift.
The core's earlier executable check passed 12 XCTest cases, including every byte
in every supported base and every increment carry reconstruction. No tests were
rerun for the mission wording edit. This document is source review, not UI runtime QA.

Bits and nibble grouping
- Display order is 7...0, weights 128,64,32,16,8,4,2,1; callbacks use LSB=0.
- For 42, the lit places sum 32+8+2, binary is 00101010, hex is 2A.
- The upper nibble 2 contributes 2*16=32; lower nibble A contributes 10.
- Increment 15 resets bits0...3 and lights bit4, yielding 00010000 / 10 / 16.
- Increment 255 resets all eight places and the explanatory text correctly says
  that 256 requires a ninth bit. Decrement zero wraps to 255.

Logic
- A=170 (10101010), B=204 (11001100): AND=136, OR=238, XOR=102, NOT A=85.
- The one-bit truth-table rows use a=index/2 and b=index%2, then mask result &1.
  NOT uses rows 0 and 1 and yields 1 and 0 despite the engine operating on a byte.
- Shifts operate on the source byte A and replace the switchboard. For 129,
  left shift yields 2 and right shift yields 64; lost outer bits are described.

Bases
- The representations and place tiles read from the same RadixNumber.
- 42 in binary=101010, octal=52, decimal=42, hex=2A. Contributions sum to 42
  in all four selections; absence of leading zeros does not change the quantity.
- Carry examples: binary111->1000, octal77->100, decimal99->100,
  hexadecimal F->10. The carryCount rule selects places 8,64,100,16 respectively.
- Borrow examples: binary1000->111, octal100->77, decimal100->99,
  hexadecimal 10->F. The borrowed place drops by 1 and trailing zeros become
  base-1. Byte boundary transitions are separately handled before these rules.
- Conversion calls the throwing parser rather than the clamping initializer,
  so inputs 256 decimal / 100 hex / 100000000 binary fail with the range explanation.

Discover and color
- Mission success validates the actual toggled answer byte against target.
- Checking saves unique mission IDs; incorrect answers do not add progress.
- RGB sliders are integer steps 0...255. RGB(103,224,196) maps to #67E0C4.
  Two hex digits represent each channel byte; FF and 00 descriptions are correct.

One real learning-flow issue
- Discover's answer is local state, separate from playground.number. Solving the
 127 mission does not put 127 into Bits, but its explanation currently says
 "Add 1 in Bits". Following that text may increment a different number.
 Recommended minimal correction: "In Bits, set 127 and add 1..."; alternatively,
 provide an explicit action to transfer the mission answer to the switchboard.
